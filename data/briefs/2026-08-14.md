## ★ Brent crude surges past $90 as OPEC extends supply cuts amid Middle East tension
**How it reaches India:** India imports about 85% of the crude oil it uses to run cars, factories, and power generators. → When oil prices go up, India's total import bill balloons (the country has to pay much more total money to buy the same amount of oil). → This creates a bigger CAD [the gap between what India earns from exports and pays for imports], which makes the rupee weaken (each rupee buys fewer dollars). → Costlier oil leads to higher fuel and transport prices inside India, pushing up overall inflation (the general rise in prices of everyday goods).
- The rupee ▼ (High, weeks) — India needs more dollars to pay for costlier oil imports.
- Gold ▲ (Medium, days) — Investors run to gold as a safe-haven asset when global tensions rise.
- Oil marketing companies like BPCL and IOC ▼ (High, weeks) — Higher raw oil purchase costs squeeze their profit margins if they cannot raise pump prices.
- Paint and tyre companies like Asian Paints and MRF ▼ (Medium, months) — Crude oil is a main raw material for making paints and rubber tyres.
- Nifty 50 (the main stock market index of top 50 Indian companies) ▼ (Medium, weeks) — Overall market sentiment gets hurt when inflation and import costs rise.
**Watch next:** Whether Middle East tensions actually disrupt physical oil shipments or just scare the markets; If the Indian government steps in to cut taxes on petrol and diesel

## Rupee slips to record low against dollar as FII outflows accelerate
**How it reaches India:** Foreign investors sell Indian stocks and demand US dollars, creating a shortage of rupees. → Because more people want dollars than rupees, the price of the dollar goes up and the rupee sinks. → A weaker rupee (each rupee buys fewer dollars) makes buying things from other countries much costlier. → India has to pay more rupees for the oil and electronics it imports, which can push up daily prices for everyone.
- The rupee (India's currency) ▼ (High, days) — heavy selling by foreign funds naturally pushes its value down against the dollar
- Crude oil imports ▲ (High, weeks) — weaker currency means India must pay more rupees to buy the same barrel of oil
- IT firms like TCS and Infosys ▲ (Medium, weeks) — they earn in dollars from foreign clients, so their overseas earnings look bigger when converted to weak rupees
- Gold prices in India ▲ (Medium, days) — India imports most of its gold, and a weak rupee makes importing it more expensive
- Overall Indian stock market (Nifty 50) ▼ (Medium, weeks) — when big foreign investors pull money out, share prices often drop due to lower demand
**Watch next:** Check if the Reserve Bank of India (our central bank that manages the country's money) steps in to sell dollars and defend the rupee; Watch daily reports on whether foreign investors are still selling Indian shares
