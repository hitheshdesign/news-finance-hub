- Bank loan and deposit interest rates ▲ (High, weeks) — When banks have less extra cash, they try to attract more money by offering slightly higher rates on fixed deposits, and they might make loans a tiny bit costlier. This is mixed: good for savers getting more interest, bad for borrowers paying higher EMIs.
- Short-term bond yield (the interest rate the government pays to borrow for a short time) ▲ (High, days) — As cash leaves the banking system, demand for short-term government securities drops, pushing their yields up. This means the government pays more to borrow short-term money.
- The rupee (vs the US dollar) ▲ (Medium, weeks) — By keeping local interest rates firm and controlling excess cash, the RBI helps protect the rupee from sliding against the US dollar (the currency used in the US).
