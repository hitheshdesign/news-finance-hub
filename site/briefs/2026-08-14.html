★ Top signal Commodities 1 source

Brent crude surges past $90 as OPEC extends supply cuts amid Middle East tension

In plain English

When global oil prices jump, India has to spend a lot more money to buy fuel from abroad, which affects our everyday costs and the Indian rupee.

Global oil prices crossed $90 a barrel because major oil-producing countries decided to keep producing less oil, while tensions in the Middle East made people worry about future supplies.

How this reaches India
  • India imports about 85% of the crude oil it uses to run cars, factories, and power generators.
  • When oil prices go up, India's total import bill balloons (the country has to pay much more total money to buy the same amount of oil).
  • This creates a bigger CAD [the gap between what India earns from exports and pays for imports], which makes the rupee weaken (each rupee buys fewer dollars).
  • Costlier oil leads to higher fuel and transport prices inside India, pushing up overall inflation (the general rise in prices of everyday goods).

Rupee slips to record low against dollar as FII outflows accelerate

In plain English

When big foreign investors pull their money out of India, our currency gets weaker, which can make imported goods like oil more expensive for us.

Foreign Institutional Investors (big money managers from other countries who invest in India) are taking their cash out of the Indian stock market and selling rupees to buy US dollars instead. This has pushed the rupee (India's money) down to its lowest value ever compared to the dollar.

How this reaches India
  • Foreign investors sell Indian stocks and demand US dollars, creating a shortage of rupees.
  • Because more people want dollars than rupees, the price of the dollar goes up and the rupee sinks.
  • A weaker rupee (each rupee buys fewer dollars) makes buying things from other countries much costlier.
  • India has to pay more rupees for the oil and electronics it imports, which can push up daily prices for everyone.
