This weather disruption leaves key summer crops like pulses (dals) and oilseeds (used to make cooking oil) short of water, damaging their growth and reducing the overall harvest.

Think of it like… It is like a dry summer spoiling your home vegetable garden, forcing you to buy all your vegetables from the expensive supermarket instead of growing them cheaply at home.
How this reaches India

  • Because rains are 15% lower than normal, farmers struggle to grow enough summer crops like pulses and oilseeds.
  • Lower crop supplies mean food becomes scarcer in markets, causing vegetable and grocery prices to rise.
  • Higher food prices pinch household budgets and force the RBI (Reserve Bank of India, the country's central bank) to keep interest rates high to control inflation.
