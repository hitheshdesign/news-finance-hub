‘Fed’ up with inflation? US rate hike leaves RBI with an October-or-December dilemma, economists say

In plain English

When the US central bank keeps its borrowing costs high, global money rushes back to America, which makes things costlier in India and pressures the Reserve Bank of India to make tough choices.

The US Federal Reserve (America's central money manager, called the Fed) decided to hike its interest rates because inflation (rising prices of everyday things) in the US refuses to cool down. When the US pays higher interest on safe investments, global investors pull their money out of developing countries like India to park it in America instead.

Think of it like… It is like a giant global bank next door suddenly offering much higher interest on savings than your local bank; naturally, everyone rushes to move their money across the street.
How this reaches India
  • The US Fed raises interest rates to fight American inflation.
  • Global investors pull their money out of Indian markets to chase higher, safer returns in America.
  • This sudden outflow of foreign money causes the Indian rupee to weaken against the US dollar.
  • A weaker rupee makes imported items like crude oil expensive, which eventually feeds into higher local inflation for everyday goods in India.
