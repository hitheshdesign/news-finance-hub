Inflation persisted in August, potentially locking in a Fed interest rate hike

In plain English

When the US central bank keeps interest rates high, money moves out of India to America, making our imports costlier and pushing up everyday prices.

Prices in America stayed stubbornly high in August, forcing the US Fed (Federal Reserve, America's central bank that controls interest rates) to push borrowing costs higher to cool things down. Prices kept rising because everyday items and services in the US are still seeing high demand from consumers.

Think of it like… It is like the biggest bank in your town suddenly offering a much higher interest rate on savings. Naturally, people start pulling their money out of smaller shops and local projects to put it safely in that big bank.
How this reaches India
  • US inflation stays high -> US Fed raises interest rates to control it.
  • US interest rates rise -> money earns more sitting safely in America.
  • Foreign investors (FIIs, big overseas investment funds) pull their money out of Indian stock markets to chase higher US returns.
  • FIIs selling Indian stocks and buying dollars makes the rupee weaken (each rupee buys fewer dollars), which makes imported items like crude oil expensive for India.
