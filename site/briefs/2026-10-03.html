Pattern from history
1979 to 1982 · United States

The cure that caused a recession on purpose

Paul Volcker, newly in charge of the US Federal Reserve, raised interest rates to nearly 20% to break an inflation that had run for a decade. He knew it would cause a severe recession. He did it anyway.

  1. Why it happened

    Through the 1970s the Fed had repeatedly raised rates, seen unemployment rise, lost its nerve and cut again. Each cycle ended with inflation higher than before. Americans had stopped believing inflation would ever be controlled, so they demanded bigger pay rises and firms raised prices pre-emptively — which made the inflation self-fulfilling. The problem was no longer economic; it was that nobody believed the promise.

  2. What it hit

    Unemployment reached nearly 11%, the worst since the Depression. Farm foreclosures and factory closures spread across the American Midwest. But inflation fell from about 14% to under 3%. The bond market, which had been a graveyard for a decade, began a bull run that lasted forty years — and shares followed it.

  3. The policy brought in to fix it

    The policy was simply to keep rates punishingly high until expectations broke, and to say so publicly and repeatedly. What it established was the doctrine that a central bank's credibility is its most valuable asset, and is worth a recession to protect.

  4. And what that policy led to

    Every inflation-fighting central bank since has followed the same script, including India's RBI and every bank that raised rates sharply in 2022. The forty-year fall in interest rates that followed also inflated the price of nearly every asset on earth — shares, bonds, property — which is a large part of why the generation that invested after 1982 did so well, and why repeating their returns from today's starting point is harder.

The pattern to rememberInflation is beaten by breaking what people expect, not by any single rate decision. That is why central bankers sound repetitive and stern: the words are the policy. When a central bank starts caring more about growth than about its own credibility, the inflation problem is about to get longer.
The India echoThe RBI made the same choice in 2013-14 under Raghuram Rajan, raising rates into a slowdown to defend the rupee and break inflation, and then adopting a formal inflation target in 2016. India's inflation has been far better behaved since — the policy worked, and the mechanism was credibility.
The modern tellCentral bankers talking about expectations becoming "unanchored". That is the technical phrase for the 1970s problem, and it signals they are prepared to tolerate real pain.
