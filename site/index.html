September 2022 · United Kingdom

A budget that broke the pension system in three days

A new British government announced large unfunded tax cuts. Within three days UK government bond yields spiked so violently that pension funds faced collapse, and the Bank of England had to step in with emergency bond buying. The Prime Minister resigned within seven weeks.

  1. Why it happened

    Pension funds had used a strategy called liability-driven investing, which borrows against bonds to match long-term obligations. It works while yields move gradually. When yields jumped, the funds faced collateral calls, and the only way to meet them was to sell the very bonds that were falling — pushing yields higher still. Hidden leverage almost nobody outside the industry had noticed.

  2. What it hit

    The pound hit an all-time low against the dollar. Mortgage rates jumped and hundreds of mortgage products were pulled within days. The government's entire fiscal plan was reversed.

  3. The policy brought in to fix it

    The Bank of England bought long-dated bonds for a strictly limited window to break the loop, then regulators forced pension funds to hold far larger liquidity buffers against exactly this scenario.

  4. And what that policy led to

    It established that a rich, stable country with its own currency can still be disciplined by the bond market within days. Finance ministers everywhere became noticeably more careful about announcing unfunded spending.

The pattern to rememberThe dangerous leverage is the leverage nobody discusses, usually because it sits inside an institution everyone considers boring. Ask who is forced to sell when prices fall — forced sellers turn a move into a spiral.
