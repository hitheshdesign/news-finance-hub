February to April 2020 · Globally

The fastest crash and the fastest rescue

Global shares fell about 35% in five weeks — the fastest crash of that size ever recorded. Then, within months, they made new highs, while the real economy was still shut.

  1. Why it happened

    A genuine shock met an economy with no slack. But the crash's speed came from its mechanics: everything was sold at once to raise cash, including gold and government bonds. The rescue's speed came from central banks having the 2008 playbook ready and choosing to use it at a scale and pace nobody expected.

  2. What it hit

    India's Nifty fell 38% in a month and recovered within the year. Oil futures briefly traded below zero. Meanwhile millions lost work and informal economies were devastated — the gap between the market and the economy was the widest most investors had ever seen.

  3. The policy brought in to fix it

    Interest rates to near zero, unlimited bond buying, and — the genuine novelty — enormous direct fiscal transfers to households and firms. Money was put into people's hands rather than only into the banking system, which is what 2008 had done.

  4. And what that policy led to

    The recovery was far faster than after 2008, and the inflation that followed in 2021-23 was far larger — the first serious inflation in the rich world in forty years, and the reason interest rates rose so sharply afterwards. It also drew a whole generation of new retail investors into markets, in India especially, whose first experience was a V-shaped recovery.

The pattern to rememberMarkets price the future, not the present. They bottomed while the news was at its worst because the rescue was visible before the recovery was. Waiting for the news to improve means buying after the move.
The India echoIndia's retail investing boom dates from this crash — SIP accounts and demat openings multiplied. Most of that money has never seen a long bear market, which is a genuine risk factor for Indian small and mid caps.
The modern tellA policy response arriving faster than the damage. When the rescue is credible and immediate, the market stops waiting for the data.
